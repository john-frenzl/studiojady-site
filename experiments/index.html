Lost PLA, found aluminum No. 02

Lost-wax casting is one of the oldest ways to make a bronze. You carve the piece in wax, bury it in a mold, melt the wax out and pour metal into the space it leaves behind. This experiment keeps the method and swaps the wax for a 3D print.

  1. The model. A bust of a woman in a winged helmet, a cat on each shoulder, set on a carved plinth. Every strand of hair and fold of cloth on screen has to survive into metal.
  2. Hollow it out. A solid bust wastes plastic, takes longer to burn away and pours a lot of aluminum. So the form becomes a thin shell, with a void at its core.
  3. Split and pin. Cut in half down the middle, each side prints flat on the bed with fewer supports. Pins along the seam lock the halves back together exactly.
  4. Print in PLA. The pattern is printed in ordinary PLA, then joined, and sprues are added to carry the metal in and let the air out.
  5. Invest and burn out. The pattern is set in a flask of investment plaster. Once cured, the flask goes into a kiln and the PLA burns away, leaving a perfect negative of the bust.
  6. Pour. Molten aluminum fills the cavity. When it cools, the plaster is broken off, the sprues are cut, and the cast is cleaned and finished by hand.

The print is lost so the metal can be found.
